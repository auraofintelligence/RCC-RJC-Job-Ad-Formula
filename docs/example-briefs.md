# Six fictional example briefs

Research checked **23 October 2026**. These are teaching examples, not actual vacancies, employer commitments, legal pay calculations or forecasts. The 2030 closing date is an obvious fixed sample date, not a claim that these rates will apply then. Every company is invented. Application contacts use reserved `example.org` addresses and paths; they are not recruitment channels. Research websites below are separate from those fictional contacts.

The ranges below are editorial choices informed by Australian pay evidence. They are **before income tax, exclude superannuation and are not independently verified offers**. Every example retains **Not yet** for the employer's pay/conditions check. Determine actual award/agreement coverage, classification, ordinary hours, penalties, overtime, allowances and super before replacing the sample with a real brief. Do not infer that a salary absorbs additional entitlements.

## Qualifications and hiring pathways

The electrician example requires a current Queensland electrical work licence authorising the work. [WorkSafe Queensland's licence guidance](https://www.worksafe.qld.gov.au/licensing-and-registrations/electrical-licences/electrical-worker-licences) is the official starting point. The sample is a qualified employee role, not an apprentice or contractor arrangement; the employer must verify licence scope and status.

For the aged-care example, Certificate III in Individual Support (Ageing) or equivalent is a **sample employer requirement for the illustrated qualified role**. The Aged Care Award's Schedule B.2.3 describes this qualified direct-care classification. It is not a claim that every aged-care job requires that certificate. New qualified entrants may apply. The sample includes paid site induction and supervised buddy shifts, not independent nursing assessment or medication administration.

The [Aged Care Quality and Safety Commission's workforce obligations](https://www.agedcarequality.gov.au/providers/workforce-obligations/about-workforce-obligations) require appropriate skills, qualifications and experience and current worker screening. Its guidance generally identifies police-certificate or NDIS worker-screening routes, depending on the circumstances, and notes future changes. Therefore the brief asks the employer to verify the current applicable checks before starting, rather than promising one fixed process. No resident details or applicant screening documents belong in this ad-writing tool.

The other pathways are stated in the fictional briefs: no-experience entry and paid induction; graduate interviews drawing on university/placement work; management interviews about team supervision; and a technical discussion plus a short paired interview exercise. These are invented process details for the examples, not claims about the cited employers, publishers or the regional labour market.
